By 2024, docs.redhat.com was live at scale—but the new experience had no evergreen way to hear from users on the page itself. I helped lead the push to change that: documenting the gap against competitors, building internal support, and co-leading the research program that shipped an on-page feedback mechanism. The team is still iterating on it.

## Grass-roots research culture

From 2022 through 2024 I was part of **PWDR** (People Who Do Research)—a grass-roots group of practitioners across Red Hat who wanted to do more customer-facing research without waiting on a central team. That network mattered. It connected me to **Red Hat Summit** research three springs in a row: in-person sessions with customers who care deeply about documentation and enterprise platforms.

Summit work fed product and marketing decisions on redhat.com. On docs, we needed something that could run **between** those annual touchpoints.

## The gap

The migrated docs.redhat.com experience was built for self-service at scale—onboarding, troubleshooting, reference—but we were behind peers on a basic pattern: lightweight, on-page feedback. Major documentation sites had long offered a “was this helpful?” or similar intercept; we didn’t.

I led the **initial internal push**: framing the competitive gap, tying it to weekday traffic on the order of tens of thousands of visitors, and arguing that ad-hoc studies alone couldn’t cover the long tail of products, versions, and page types. Advocacy and proof, not a solo implementation.

## Into a funded research program

The idea graduated from hallway conversations into formal **Digital Experience Research (DXR)** planning. DocX Feedback Mechanism became a **2024 Key Research Project** on the new docs.redhat.com experience.

**Research leads:** Wes Carr and Jess Sturgis. **Collaborators:** DocX, DXR, and CCS.

**Objective:** create an evergreen, static on-page feedback mechanism to gather insights about users’ experience with the interface, content, and features.

**Method:** Qualtrics on-page intercept—user-initiated, in context.

**Audience:** docs users in builder and newbie personas—people engaging with product documentation to learn, troubleshoot, and explore.

**Launch:** September–October 2024, with follow-up readouts on a roughly 90-day cadence.

## Decisions

| Choice | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| On-page intercept | Feedback tied to the page someone is actually on |
| Evergreen / static placement | Signal without waiting for a release to ask the question |
| User-initiated | Captures intent when someone chooses to respond |
| Qualitative + trend readouts | Identify patterns in goals, expectations, and pain—not just a single launch metric |

Early priorities: user-initiated feedback on interface and content, and spotting opportunities and trends across responses.

## Outcomes and what’s next

The mechanism shipped on docs.redhat.com. Readouts feed DocX, platform UX, and content stakeholders—same ecosystem as the wider [Unified Learn](/work-preview/red-hat/unified-learn/) platform work.

It’s live, not finished: placement, copy, routing, and how teams act on responses are still evolving. That’s appropriate for a surface that will keep growing content types and products.
